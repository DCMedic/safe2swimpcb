Bring data to the beach bag

Plan a Panama City Beach vacation with historical beach conditions.

Choosing travel dates is usually about schedules, prices and sunshine. Safe2Swim PCB adds another layer: what beach-flag conditions were observed around similar calendar dates across a historical record reaching back to 2017?

Open Safe2Swim PCB and choose Vacation Planner

What the Vacation Planner does

The planner compares a future trip window with comparable dates in the historical Panama City Beach record. It reports observed frequencies for yellow, single-red and double-red conditions, together with sample size and source context. Purple frequencies use only dates where Purple reporting is known to be reliable.

Where the history comes from

Primary ALERTBAY/PCBFLAGS and Safe2Swim observations remain the highest-priority evidence. The record is extended with audited National Weather Service Bay-flag reports only on dates where primary data are missing, giving the planner a much larger historical sample without silently overwriting the original archive.

What the planner does not do

It does not forecast the flag for a future vacation. Weather systems, tropical activity and local surf conditions vary from year to year. A historical frequency is a planning statistic, not a promise that a particular future week will be calm or hazardous.

How to use the result well

  • Compare several possible travel windows rather than looking for a single “perfect” week.
  • Pay attention to sample size and source coverage, not just the percentage.
  • Use historical environmental context such as wind, wave and tide data as supporting information.
  • Once your trip arrives, switch completely to today's posted beach flag and official local guidance.

Why the planner improves over time

Safe2Swim now combines multiple years of audited historical observations with continuing automatic collection. New observations and finalized environmental records continue to expand the evidence base, while source provenance remains visible for research and quality control.
