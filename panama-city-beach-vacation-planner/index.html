Bring data to the beach bag

Plan a Panama City Beach vacation with historical beach conditions.

Choosing travel dates is usually about schedules, prices and sunshine. Safe2Swim PCB adds another layer: what beach-flag conditions were observed around similar calendar dates in the historical archive?

Open Safe2Swim PCB and choose Vacation Planner

What the Vacation Planner does

The planner compares a future trip window with comparable dates in the historical Panama City Beach archive. It reports observed frequencies for yellow, single-red, double-red and purple conditions, together with sample size so you can see how much evidence supports the number.

What the planner does not do

It does not forecast the flag for a future vacation. Weather systems, tropical activity and local surf conditions vary from year to year. A historical frequency is a planning statistic, not a promise that a particular future week will be calm or hazardous.

How to use the result well

  • Compare several possible travel windows rather than looking for a single “perfect” week.
  • Pay attention to sample size and coverage, not just the percentage.
  • Use historical environmental context such as wind, wave and tide data as supporting information.
  • Once your trip arrives, switch completely to today's posted beach flag and official local guidance.

Why the planner improves over time

The original Safe2Swim PCB archive covers hundreds of observed beach-flag days, and the site is configured to append new current observations automatically. As additional years accumulate, comparisons around the same calendar dates become more informative and more useful for future predictive-model research.
