The Gulf leaves a pattern

Panama City Beach flag history.

Safe2Swim PCB preserves historical Panama City Beach flag observations instead of showing only today's status. The result is a growing public dataset for vacation planning, pattern discovery and future predictive-model research.

Open Historical Patterns on Safe2Swim PCB

What is in the archive?

The original Safe2Swim PCB dataset was built from hundreds of ALERTBAY beach-flag messages spanning 2024 through 2026. Those observations were normalized into a timestamped message table and a daily summary table while preserving the original evidence separately.

Future automated observations are appended rather than rewriting the historical archive. That design makes it possible to distinguish supplied source records from later machine-collected data.

What patterns can history reveal?

Persistence

Elevated beach hazards often occur in episodes rather than isolated moments. Safe2Swim tracks how frequently red-or-worse days are followed by another elevated day.

Intraday changes

Some days receive multiple alerts. Recording the individual updates preserves the fact that a morning condition can change later in the day.

Seasonal context

Monthly and date-window summaries can help vacation planners compare historical observations without treating a short archive as long-term climatology.

Environmental associations

Weather, wind, waves, swell and tide features can be joined to the flag record to study which combinations are associated with elevated flags.

Why Safe2Swim keeps raw, daily and modeling data separate

The raw observation table answers “what alert was recorded and when?” The daily summary answers “what happened on this date?” The modeling table answers a different question: “which finalized environmental features can be compared statistically with the flag outcome?” Keeping those layers separate reduces accidental data leakage and makes the research more auditable.
