Beach safety guide

Florida beach flag meanings.

Florida’s statewide beach warning system gives visitors a fast visual cue about surf hazards and dangerous marine life. Flags are an important safety signal, but they do not replace lifeguard instructions, posted closures or local beach rules.

What each Florida beach flag means

Green — low hazardGenerally calm conditions. Low hazard never means zero hazard; continue to use caution.
Yellow — medium hazardModerate surf and/or currents. Use extra caution, especially with children and weaker swimmers.
Red — high hazardHigh surf and/or strong currents. Conditions can be dangerous even for experienced swimmers.
Double red — water closedWater access is closed to the public under the local beach-safety authority. Follow posted orders and lifeguards.
Purple — dangerous marine lifeDangerous marine life is present. Purple commonly flies together with another surf-condition flag.

Flags are not a rip-current forecast

The Florida Department of Environmental Protection explains that beach flags give a general warning about overall surf conditions; they do not specifically announce the presence of a rip current. A green or yellow flag therefore should never be interpreted as proof that rip currents are absent.

Before entering the Gulf, check the posted flag, ask a lifeguard about local conditions and review the National Weather Service beach or rip-current forecast for the area. Learn how to respond if you are caught in one in our rip-current safety guide.

Why local conditions still control

Florida provides the standardized warning system, while local governments and beach-safety agencies install, maintain and operate flags. Conditions can change during the day, and the official flag at a particular beach may change after a forecast was issued. The posted local flag and instructions from lifeguards or public-safety personnel should always take priority.
